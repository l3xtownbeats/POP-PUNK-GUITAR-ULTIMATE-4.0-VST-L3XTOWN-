# California Pop-Punk Sound Design Target — PPGU 5.0

Date: 2026-11-11
Status: DESIGN TARGET / implementation and audio validation required.

## Product goal

The primary sound of POP PUNK GUITAR ULTIMATE is a convincing, mix-ready California pop-punk guitar bank: fast, tight, bright, punchy and melodic, with enough body in the low mids and controlled upper-mid bite to stay powerful without becoming fizzy. The target is the production language associated with the Jerry Finn-era pop-punk records and the playing/arrangement vocabulary around Blink-182 (Tom DeLonge and Mark Hoppus), Travis Barker's fast, precise rhythmic feel, Avril Lavigne and Simple Plan.

Artist, producer and track names are style references only. The plugin must not claim to recreate an exact artist's rig, master recording, performance, guitar, amp preset or isolated stem. No commercial record audio or proprietary samples are to be copied.

## What the references mean for this guitar instrument

- **Jerry Finn production aesthetic:** tightly edited and layered rhythm guitars, clear transient definition, controlled low end, strong but not harsh upper mids, wide chorus layers and a polished, dense mix. Treat this as a mix/arrangement target, not a single amp tone.
- **Tom DeLonge / Blink-182 guitar vocabulary:** assertive downstrokes, compact power-chord voicings, bright mid-forward distortion, tight palm-muted verse parts, broad open choruses, octave hooks and short melodic fills.
- **Mark Hoppus context:** bass is not a guitar sample target. Use this reference only to keep guitar voicings out of the bass register and leave a deliberate low-frequency pocket; do not label bass sounds as guitar presets.
- **Travis Barker context:** drummer reference only. Translate the fast, precise, stop-start rhythmic energy into Pattern timing, accents, rests, note lengths and tight palm-muted/open-chord transitions. Do not claim to reproduce drum performances.
- **Avril Lavigne / Simple Plan:** bright, catchy, polished melodic pop-punk rhythm and lead tones; clean-to-crunch contrasts, clear chord attacks, octave melodies and energetic open choruses.

## SoundBank priorities

The 10 GB ceiling is a maximum budget, not a target to fill. Prioritize unique, high-quality multisamples and articulation coverage over duplicated libraries or sheer file count.

1. **Core electric rhythm:** clean DI and amp-coloured samples suitable for controlled amp/cab processing; open power-chord voicings; velocity layers; down/up stroke variation where the source actually provides it.
2. **Palm mute:** dedicated muted recordings/zones at several strengths and pitches. Do not synthesize a palm-mute label from an ordinary open note. If source samples do not contain the articulation, use a clearly marked DSP envelope/filter fallback and identify it as synthesized.
3. **Open/crunch/distortion:** at least separate clean, crunch and high-gain source profiles, with consistent level matching and no destructive clipping. Distortion should remain chord-readable.
4. **Lead:** single-note chromatic coverage, sustained notes, short/staccato notes, slides/legato only where source recordings or implemented DSP genuinely support them; octave and power-chord voicings must be mapped as real multisample zones or generated by documented note logic.
5. **Acoustic contrast:** nylon/steel acoustic strumming and picked patterns for verse/bridge/intro use, not as a substitute for the electric core.
6. **Performance realism:** note-off/release behavior, round-robin only where alternate samples exist, velocity response, humanized timing/accents and sample-start consistency.
7. **Layering:** left/right double-track presets should use independent takes or safe variation where available; do not duplicate one mono sample and describe it as a real double-track.

## Mix-ready starting targets (adjustable per preset)

These are starting ranges for audition and tuning, not mandatory fixed EQ values:
- High-pass / low-frequency cleanup: usually 70–110 Hz depending on tuning and arrangement.
- Low-mid control: inspect 180–350 Hz for boxiness; preserve enough body for power chords.
- Pick / presence definition: inspect 1.5–3.5 kHz; avoid brittle excessive 4–7 kHz.
- High shelf / fizz: use cabinet filtering and restrained top-end roll-off rather than masking with heavy EQ.
- Rhythm distortion: moderate gain with chord separation; use less gain than a solo sound.
- Stereo: use genuinely different takes/round robins for wide doubles; keep a mono-compatible centre option.
- Effects: short room/plate reverb, tempo delay and restrained chorus/flanger as optional presets, not always-on wash.

## Acceptance tests

1. Blind-audition the core rhythm presets against licensed reference mixes at matched loudness; judge attack, chord definition, low-mid weight, fizz and decay.
2. Test palm-muted eighth notes and fast downstrokes at several velocities across low/mid/high registers.
3. Test open chorus chords, single-note melodies, octave hooks and acoustic picked/strummed patterns.
4. Verify that all claimed articulations exist in the source maps or are explicitly identified as DSP-generated.
5. Check phase/mono compatibility, peak headroom, CPU, voice stealing and sample streaming.
6. Confirm every factory preset points to a valid SoundBank map and can produce audio in the VST3; metadata-only presets must remain marked incomplete.

## Legal and technical boundaries

Only bundle samples whose actual license and redistribution terms permit inclusion in the plugin's SoundBank. Preserve license files, source attribution, provenance and SHA-256 hashes. Do not scrape commercial stems, isolated tracks, guitar tabs/transcriptions, NI content, or paid sample packs. A reference artist/track is a tonal target, never a source recording.
